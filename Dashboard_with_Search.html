📚 Understanding Compa-Ratio (CR)

A Simple Guide to Market Competitiveness for Everyone

🤔 What is Compa-Ratio (CR)?

Compa-Ratio =
A Colleague's Actual Salary Market Median Salary

Think of it this way: If the market median salary for your position is $60,000 per year, and you earn $60,000, your CR is 1.0 — you're right at the market midpoint!

💡 Real-Life Examples

CR = 0.8 Below Market

Market Median: $60,000/year

Actual Salary: $48,000/year

$48,000 ÷ $60,000 = 0.8

💭 Salary is 20% below market median. Consider review.

CR = 1.0 Market Median

Market Median: $60,000/year

Actual Salary: $60,000/year

$60,000 ÷ $60,000 = 1.0

💭 Salary is exactly at market midpoint. Target for fully competent associate.

CR = 1.2 Above Market

Market Median: $60,000/year

Actual Salary: $72,000/year

$72,000 ÷ $60,000 = 1.2

💭 Salary is 20% above market median. High performer or tenure.

🎯 Why is CR Important?

📏

When Market Data is Limited

Sometimes we don't have full market range data (P10, P25, P75, P90). CR is a simple, powerful indicator that works with just one number: the market median.

⚖️

Fairness Check

CR helps ensure colleagues are paid fairly relative to the market. It's not about absolute numbers — it's about relative position.

💰

Budget Planning

CR tells us exactly how much budget is needed to bring underpaid colleagues to market level. No guessing!

🌍

Global Comparison

CR normalizes different currencies and markets, making it easy to compare across hotels and countries.

🎓 Remember These Key Points

1

CR = 1.0 means salary is exactly at market median — this is the target for fully competent colleagues.

2

CR < 0.8 means salary is more than 20% below market — review needed.

3

CR > 1.2 means salary is more than 20% above market — high performer or long tenure.

4

Expected range is 0.8 - 1.2 — within this range, salary is generally aligned with market.

5

CR works even with limited data — only need market median, not full range.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Does CR mean I'm underpaid if it's below 1.0?
A: Not necessarily! CR 0.8 - 1.0 is still within the acceptable range. It depends on your experience, tenure, and performance. New hires often start below 1.0.
Q: Can CR be above 1.2?
A: Yes! CR above 1.2 usually indicates a high performer, someone with long tenure, or a specialized skill set that's in high demand.
Q: Why use market median, not average?
A: Median is less affected by extreme values (very high or very low salaries). It represents the "middle point" where half earn more and half earn less.
Q: Is CR the same as performance rating?
A: No! CR is about market position, not performance. However, high performers often have higher CR due to merit increases over time.

📖 How to Read What-If Analysis

  • Total Salary Budget Increase (%): Drag the slider to simulate different budget increase scenarios (e.g., 3% = hotel decides to increase total salary pool by 3%)
  • Low CR Weight: Higher value means more budget is allocated to employees with Compa-Ratio < 0.8 (below market). 2.0x means they get double the average allocation
  • High Performer Weight: Higher value means top performers get more than average share of the budget
  • Allocation Matrix: Shows how the budget is distributed across categories based on the weights you set
  • Projected New CR: Estimates what the average Compa-Ratio would be after applying this budget allocation
